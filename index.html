    Eligibility

    Requirements

    To join the South African Police Service as a trainee constable or civilian employee, applicants must meet the following minimum criteria.

    General requirements
    • South African citizen with valid ID document or smart card
    • Minimum age of 18 years; maximum age of 30 years at date of application
    • Matric / Grade 12 certificate or equivalent NQF Level 4 qualification
    • No prior criminal record or pending criminal charges
    • Proficiency in at least two official languages of South Africa
    • Valid driver's licence (Code B minimum) is advantageous
    • Must be willing to be deployed anywhere in South Africa
    Physical and medical
    • Pass a comprehensive medical examination by a SAPS-approved doctor
    • Pass a physical fitness assessment (includes 2.4km run, sit-ups, push-ups)
    • Eyesight correctable to 6/6 with lenses; no colour blindness
    • No physical disability that would prevent execution of policing duties
    • Psychological fitness assessment and psychometric evaluation
    • Drug and alcohol screening test, with negative results required
    • Fingerprint clearance and security vetting process

    SAPS is an equal opportunity employer and encourages applications from women, persons with disabilities and all designated groups as defined in the Employment Equity Act. Preference will be given to previously disadvantaged individuals in line with the SAPS Employment Equity Plan.

    How to apply

    Application process

    From your first application to your first day on patrol, here is the complete journey to becoming a SAPS officer.

    1
    Online application
    Complete the Z83 application form online or at your nearest police station. Attach certified copies of your ID, Matric certificate and any additional qualifications.
    Deadline: rolling admissions
    2
    Screening and shortlisting
    Your application is reviewed against minimum requirements. Shortlisted candidates are notified via email and SMS within 8 weeks of the application closing date.
    Duration: 6 to 8 weeks
    3
    Fitness and medical testing
    Attend your designated test centre for a physical fitness assessment, full medical examination, psychometric testing and drug screening.
    Duration: 1 to 2 days
    4
    Vetting and background checks
    SAPS conducts thorough criminal record checks, security vetting and reference verification. All results are treated as confidential.
    Duration: 4 to 6 weeks
    5
    Acceptance and placement
    Successful candidates receive a formal offer of placement at one of the SAPS training colleges across South Africa.
    Notification: written offer letter
    6
    Basic training college
    Complete a 6 month residential training programme covering law, firearms, tactical policing, community relations and physical training.
    Duration: 6 months residential

    Detective services

    Criminal investigation

    SAPS Detective Services investigates all crime categories, from contact crime to complex financial fraud, using modern forensic and investigative methods.

    The Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation (DPCI), commonly known as the Hawks, and the broader SAPS Detective Services division form the backbone of criminal investigation in South Africa. Their mandate is to investigate, prevent and combat all forms of serious, organised and priority crime.

    Detectives work in close collaboration with forensic experts, prosecutors from the National Prosecuting Authority and international partners like Interpol to build cases that hold up in court.

    Specialised units

    The Hawks (DPCI)
    Investigates serious organised crime, corruption and complex financial crimes at a national level.
    FCS Unit
    Family Violence, Child Protection and Sexual Offences: dedicated specialists for gender-based and child crimes.
    Cybercrime Unit
    Tackles digital fraud, online scams, dark web activity, ransomware and cyberterrorism.
    LCRC (Forensics)
    Local Criminal Record Centre handles crime scene processing, fingerprinting and evidence management.
    Commercial Crime
    Fraud, money laundering, tax evasion, Ponzi schemes and corporate corruption investigations.
    Organised Crime
    Gang violence, drug syndicates, human trafficking and cross-border criminal networks.

    How to assist an investigation

    • Preserve evidence: do not move or touch items at a crime scene
    • Write down everything you remember as soon as possible after the incident
    • Obtain witness contact details if it is safe to do so
    • Report via the 10111 emergency line or visit your nearest station
    • Use the anonymous Crime Stop tip line: 08600 10111

    Patrol and response

    Visible policing

    Uniformed officers are the most immediate face of the SAPS: on patrol, in your streets and first on scene. Their presence deters crime and provides immediate community safety.

    Visible Policing forms the operational frontline of SAPS. Officers in uniform conduct foot patrols, vehicle patrols, roadblocks and rapid response operations across all 1,154 police stations in South Africa.

    The sector policing model divides communities into manageable geographic sectors, each assigned a dedicated team of officers who get to know the residents, local businesses and recurring crime patterns in their area.

    What we do

    • 24/7 patrol operations across all nine provinces
    • Rapid response to emergency calls received via 10111
    • Roadblocks and vehicle checkpoints to combat vehicle crime
    • Sector policing with dedicated officers per community zone
    • Crime prevention operations, raids and stop-and-search actions
    • Crowd management and public order policing at events
    • Border policing at ports of entry and land borders

    Specialised units

    K9 Unit
    Trained police dogs for drug detection, tracking suspects and crowd control. Highly effective in search operations.
    Air Wing
    Helicopter surveillance and pursuit operations supporting ground units across provinces.
    Reaction Unit
    High-speed motorcycle and vehicle rapid response teams for urban hot spots.
    Marine Unit
    Patrols South Africa's coastline, rivers and harbours to prevent smuggling and maritime crime.

    The National Rural Safety Strategy deploys visible policing teams to farming communities and rural areas to address farm attacks, stock theft and rural violence, ensuring no community is left behind.

    See something, say something
    10111
    Call immediately if you witness a crime in progress, a suspicious person or an emergency situation.
    Sector policing
    Each SAPS station divides its jurisdiction into sectors. Your sector officer is your first point of contact. Visit your local station to find out who your sector officer is and how to reach them directly.
    Officer identification
    All SAPS officers on duty must carry and present their SAPS appointment certificate upon request. Uniform officers display their force number on their epaulettes at all times.
    Complaints about officers
    Complaints about officer conduct are handled by the Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID). Contact IPID on 0800 587 743 (toll-free).

    Prevention through knowledge

    Crime intelligence

    Crime Intelligence is the SAPS covert arm: gathering information, disrupting threats before they materialise and supporting investigations with actionable intelligence.

    The SAPS Crime Intelligence Division operates under the strictest confidentiality. Its primary mission is to collect, collate, evaluate and analyse crime-related information to provide intelligence products that support crime prevention, detection and the disruption of criminal networks.

    Intelligence operatives work across all crime categories, from gang-related violence in the Cape Flats to cross-border drug syndicates and domestic terrorism threats. The division also supports the priority crime investigation work of the Hawks.

    The intelligence cycle

    01
    Collection
    Information is gathered through human sources, technical surveillance, tip lines, open-source monitoring and inter-agency cooperation.
    02
    Processing and collation
    Raw information is processed, cross-referenced and collated into structured intelligence reports by trained analysts.
    03
    Analysis and evaluation
    Analysts assess credibility, reliability and implications of intelligence. Threat assessments and crime threat analyses are produced.
    04
    Dissemination
    Intelligence products are shared with commanders, detectives and relevant partner agencies to enable targeted operations.
    05
    Feedback and review
    Outcomes of operations are fed back to improve future intelligence collection and analysis processes.

    Rights and justice

    Legal services

    Understanding your rights within the South African justice system. SAPS Legal Services provides guidance, oversight and access to justice for all citizens.

    South Africa has one of the most progressive constitutions in the world. The Bill of Rights enshrined in Chapter 2 of the Constitution guarantees every person fundamental rights, including rights related to arrest, detention and fair treatment by the police.

    Your rights when arrested

    • The right to remain silent and to be informed of this right
    • The right to have a legal practitioner of your choice present
    • The right to be informed promptly of the reason for your arrest
    • The right to be brought before a court within 48 hours
    • The right not to be tortured, treated cruelly or degraded
    • The right to communicate with and be visited by your spouse, partner, relative or medical practitioner
    • The right to a fair and public trial before an ordinary court

    Filing a complaint against SAPS

    Complaints of misconduct, abuse of power or criminal conduct by SAPS members are investigated independently by the Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID). IPID has the mandate to investigate deaths in custody, torture, rape, corruption and other serious misconduct.

    You have the right to lodge a complaint even if you are currently in custody or on bail. Retaliation against a complainant by any SAPS member is a criminal offence under the SAPS Act.

    Legal Aid South Africa

    If you cannot afford a private attorney, Legal Aid South Africa provides free legal representation to qualifying individuals in criminal matters. Contact Legal Aid on 0800 110 110 (toll-free) or visit your nearest Legal Aid office.

    Bail information
    Bail applications are heard in the magistrate's court. Schedule 1 offences allow for bail at station level. Schedule 5 and 6 offences require a court hearing. An attorney can guide you on the bail process.

    Together we are safer

    Community policing

    Community Police Forums (CPFs) are the bridge between SAPS and the public: building trust, sharing information and co-creating safety strategies at the neighbourhood level.

    Section 18 of the South African Police Service Act (68 of 1995) mandates the establishment of a Community Police Forum (CPF) for every police station in South Africa. CPFs consist of ordinary community members who work alongside their local station commander to shape policing priorities in their area.

    CPFs are not a vigilante group or policing unit. They are a structured community oversight and partnership body that holds the police accountable while also supporting their work.

    What CPFs do

    • Meet monthly with the station commander to discuss crime trends and policing plans
    • Represent community concerns and feedback to station management
    • Assist in planning and supporting crime prevention campaigns
    • Organise neighbourhood watch programmes and community patrols
    • Facilitate conflict resolution between the community and SAPS
    • Monitor the effectiveness of police service delivery
    • Establish sub-forums for schools, businesses and youth

    How to join or start a CPF

    01
    Contact your station
    Visit your nearest SAPS station and ask for the CPF chairperson or the community liaison officer. They will provide details about existing CPF meetings in your area.
    02
    Attend a CPF meeting
    CPF meetings are open to all community members. Meeting schedules are posted at the station notice board and on the local station's social media pages.
    03
    Register as a member
    Fill in a CPF membership form. There is no cost to join. All community members aged 18 and over are eligible regardless of background.
    04
    Stand for election
    CPF executive committees are elected annually by the community. Any registered CPF member in good standing may stand for an executive position.
    CPFs nationwide
    1,154
    Active Community Police Forums, one for every registered SAPS station in South Africa.
    Neighbourhood watch
    Neighbourhood Watch structures operate under the CPF and coordinate voluntary community patrols, CCTV monitoring and communication networks to deter crime at street level.
    School safety programme
    SAPS partners with the Department of Education to station Resource Officers at schools, conduct drug awareness programmes and address bullying and gang recruitment targeting learners.
    Business Against Crime SA
    BACSA is a public-private partnership through which businesses contribute resources, technology and expertise to support SAPS crime prevention efforts. Contact: 011 883 0717

    Official documentation

    Police clearance certificates

    A Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) is an official document confirming whether or not you have a criminal record in South Africa. Required for employment, immigration, adoption and travel.

    The SAPS Criminal Record Centre (CRC) in Pretoria is responsible for issuing Police Clearance Certificates. The certificate reflects your criminal history (or absence thereof) based on fingerprint records held by SAPS.

    PCCs are required for employment (especially in security, finance, education and government), visa and immigration applications, inter-country adoption and professional licensing by regulatory bodies.

    Application process

    01
    Complete the application form
    Download the SAPS 91(a) form from the SAPS website or collect it from any police station. Complete all sections clearly in block letters using black ink.
    02
    Get fingerprints taken
    Visit any SAPS station to have your fingerprints taken on a SAPS fingerprint form. Some private providers are also accredited for PCC fingerprints. Cost: free at SAPS stations.
    03
    Pay the application fee
    Payment of R130.00 is required via EFT or postal order payable to "SAPS". Proof of payment must accompany your application. No cash accepted by post.
    04
    Submit your application
    Post your completed form, fingerprint form, certified ID copy and proof of payment to: Criminal Record Centre, Private Bag X308, Pretoria, 0001.
    05
    Track your application
    Processing takes 20 to 30 working days for local applications. International applications may take 6 to 8 weeks. Track via email: crc@saps.gov.za
    Local: 20 to 30 working days · International: 6 to 8 weeks
    06
    Receive your certificate
    The certificate is mailed to the address provided on your application form. International applications can be collected at a South African embassy or consulate.

    Beware of scammers. Some people offer to process police clearance certificates for large fees. The official application process is managed exclusively by the SAPS Criminal Record Centre. Always use the official SAPS postal address or visit a registered SAPS station.
